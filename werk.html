Mission & Vision

Why WERK
Exists

Too many individuals with disabilities — especially those with significant support needs, and those who also identify as LGBTQ+ — are left out of meaningful workforce conversations. Systems fail them. Schools underestimate them. Employers overlook them.

The WERK Lab exists to change that. We conduct research that centers the voices of transition-age youth and adults with disabilities, train the next generation of special educators and rehabilitation counselors, and develop practical tools that help practitioners do their jobs better.

Our work sits at the intersection of vocational assessment, self-determination, disability identity, and workforce equity — and we believe that when individuals with disabilities thrive at work, communities are stronger for it.

What We Study

Research Focus
Areas

01

Vocational Assessment

Examining how vocational assessments are used — and misused — for transition-age students with significant support needs, and building better frameworks for practitioners.

02

Self-Determination & Advocacy

Supporting individuals with disabilities to develop the skills, knowledge, and opportunities to direct their own career paths and advocate for their rights.

03

Disability & LGBTQ+ Identities

Exploring the intersection of disability and LGBTQ+ identity in education, employment, and community life — centering voices that are too often invisible.

04

Employer Practices

Understanding what successful employers do differently when hiring and retaining employees with disabilities — and spreading those practices.

05

Ethics in ABA Practice

Investigating ethical dimensions of Applied Behavior Analysis in schools and clinical settings, with a focus on culturally responsive and affirming practice.

06

Caregiver & Family Perspectives

Amplifying the perspectives of families and caregivers navigating transition systems alongside their loved ones with significant disabilities.
